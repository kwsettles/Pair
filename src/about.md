PAIR stands for Parliamentary AI Review. Pronounced like “pear”, it is a research blog about AI in parliamentary practice.

I study how AI becomes part of parliamentary work. This blog gives me a place to document developments between research papers.

A new tool or pilot can be worth recording long before there is enough evidence for a journal article. I want to keep those observations in one place, with sources that readers can follow and an honest account of what we still do not know.

The intended readers are researchers, parliamentary staff and anyone interested in how these institutions change. The focus is on concrete uses: what a system does, who works with it, where its output enters a procedure and who is responsible for checking it.

## What you can expect

Short reports on developments, fuller profiles of individual systems and occasional comparisons across cases. I will publish when there is something worth documenting and enough evidence to say something useful. This is a selective research record, not a complete inventory or a daily news service.

## Formats

**Brief.** A short report on one development: a new application, a change in deployment status, a material change in workflow or a governance decision. Each Brief answers the six questions set out in the [Method](/method/#six-questions-for-every-brief).

**Case Profile.** A profile of one system that is updated as new evidence appears. It carries a stable case ID, a structured summary of the system and its status, and a version history.

**Comparative Note.** An argument across several cases. Longer than a Brief and closer to a working paper.

**Editorial.** Notes on the project itself and on changes to its methods.

**Case Register.** A table of all documented systems with their deployment status and the date that status was last verified, available for download as CSV and JSON. See the [Case Register](/cases/).

## Who is responsible?

I’m Kevin W. Settles, a researcher working on parliaments, representation and AI. This is a personal research project. It does not speak on behalf of my employer or the institutions discussed here.

Selection, interpretation and the decision to publish are my responsibility. “Review” refers to reporting and analysis. The contributions do not undergo formal peer review and are not journal articles. Where an account relies on an institution’s own description, readers should be able to see that.

## How AI is used

AI is part of the workflow behind this project. ChatGPT has been used to build the initial website and draft its initial editorial text, including this page. These texts are still drafts awaiting my review.

The current version of the website was built with Claude (Anthropic, Claude Opus 5.5) from a written specification that includes the editorial texts. Claude implemented the design, pages, navigation, local search, the case register, the citation features, support for several authors, the newsletter pages, the editing setup and the placeholder pages for the legal notice and privacy information. It drafted the sections on formats, contributors, relationships and access, funding, licence, preservation, newsletter and contact on this page, the Contribute and Newsletter pages, the evidence labels on the Method page, and the notes on its own involvement. Kevin has not yet reviewed this implementation or these sections.

For future contributions, AI may help find sources, organise material, produce preliminary summaries or translations, and draft or edit text. Each contribution will state the substantive assistance actually used. AI output will not be treated as a source or as independent confirmation of a claim.

Before a contribution is made public, I will check factual claims against the cited material, review quotations and any translations used, and take responsibility for the final argument and wording. If a source cannot be checked, that limitation will be stated. Drafts will not be described as reviewed before that review has happened.

The proposed rule for unpublished interviews and other confidential research material is to keep it out of external AI tools unless the consent and data arrangements explicitly permit that use.

## How this will appear on a post

A short note at the end will identify the tool, the tasks it assisted with and the review status. It should say what happened on that contribution rather than repeat a vague label such as “AI assisted”.

## Corrections and citation

Substantive corrections will be dated and explained on the relevant contribution. Please cite the original material for an institution’s own claims, and this blog for its analysis or synthesis. Public versions will include a suggested citation; archival DOIs are a later step.

Each published contribution ends with a suggested citation and a BibTeX entry. Paragraphs are numbered for pinpoint references. Entries in the Case Register are cited by case ID and the date of access.

## Preservation

Case Profiles, Comparative Notes and dated states of the Case Register are to be archived with version-specific DOIs on Zenodo. No DOI has been registered yet. No ISSN has been registered.
