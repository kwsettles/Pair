## Scope and selection

The proposed focus is AI used by parliaments, their administrations and identifiable parliamentary services. Executive-government tools belong within scope only when their direct relationship to parliamentary work is established and clearly labelled.

A Brief should document a substantive development: a new application, a transition in deployment status, a material change in workflow or a governance decision. Repeated announcements without new evidence do not warrant a separate report. Coverage is selective and should not be read as a census of global adoption.

## Six questions for every Brief

1. **What changed?** Record the event date separately from the source’s publication date and the note’s date.
2. **Who and what task?** Identify the institution, actors, immediate task and relevant parliamentary function.
3. **What is its status?** Distinguish announcement, pilot, operational use and discontinuation. Use “unclear” when evidence is insufficient.
4. **What supports the account?** Link the specific sources supporting each substantive claim.
5. **Why does it matter?** Explain the potential institutional relevance and label interpretation as interpretation.
6. **What remains unknown?** State the limits of the evidence and questions for follow-up.

## Sources and verification

Prioritise parliamentary documentation, published technical material, procurement records and attributable statements. Primary sources can establish what an institution says; they do not automatically establish whether claimed benefits have been realised. Supplier accounts must be labelled and checked against institutional documentation where possible.

Use interviews only where publication is compatible with the consent obtained. Keep confidential research material out of public notes. AI-assisted discovery or drafting never replaces source verification and editorial responsibility.

## Evidence labels

Every Case Profile and every entry in the Case Register carries one deployment status:

- **Announced.** The institution has stated that it intends to use the system. There is no evidence of use.
- **Pilot.** The system is in use, but limited in time, scope or user group and described as a test.
- **Operational.** The system is used in regular work.
- **Discontinued.** Use has ended.
- **Unclear.** The available evidence does not allow one of the other categories.

Every source is labelled with its type:

- **Institutional.** Documents and statements of the parliament or its administration.
- **Supplier.** Statements by the developer or vendor of the system.
- **Media.** Journalistic reporting.
- **Interview.** Interviews, used only with consent for publication.
- **PAIR inference.** A conclusion the editor draws from the cited material, marked as such.

A status that rests only on supplier or media sources is marked accordingly in the case summary.

## AI assistance and human review

AI may assist with discovery, organisation, summaries, translation and writing. Any substantive use will be disclosed on the contribution. The editor must check the underlying sources and approve the interpretation before public publication. Automated discovery does not authorise automated publication.

## Connecting notes and cases

Each documented system should receive a stable case identifier, shared across related notes, its case profile and the research dataset. Record the evidence supporting each coding decision. A new source may update an existing case rather than create a new one.

Case IDs follow the pattern PAIR-[ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 country code]-[chamber abbreviation]-[three-digit number]. An ID is never reused, even if a case is merged or withdrawn.

## Citation and corrections

Public contributions should carry an author, date, stable URL, version number and suggested citation. Substantive changes should appear in a visible version history. Archived editions with version-specific DOIs are planned for substantial profiles and research notes; no DOI has been registered for this preview.

Readers should cite the original source for an original institutional claim, and PAIR for its reconstruction, comparison or analysis. Contributions are editorial research outputs, not peer-reviewed journal articles.

## Publication checklist

Sources opened and checked · Dates distinguished · Deployment status supported · Interpretation labelled · Unknowns stated · Public-use permissions respected · Citation and revision history complete
